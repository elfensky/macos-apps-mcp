Opening the app after an update restarts the daemon — PR #625, merged; part of #568.

# Phase 05.12 Plan 03: The app after an update

## Result

- An open reads the daemon's version and build with `GET /identity` on the unix socket. When another daemon answers, it sends one `launchctl kickstart -k` and waits for a real answer for at most 300 s, with one notice that ends by itself.
- A kickstart is sent only on a definite HTTP answer from another daemon. Every transport error, and a read that passes its total 2 s bound, means "nothing answers" and sends no kickstart.
- `install-agent` waits for any answer (up to 300 s), compares nothing and sends no kickstart.
- FIX-27 is not complete here: its device half is plan 09 (M10 to M12). #568 stays open.

## Evidence

- Gate on the merged head (rebased on the other two lanes): 4238 passed; read-only tier 4223 passed, 15 skipped; ruff clean; CI `check` and `linux-tools-list` pass.
- The six `abandoned` transport tests pass unchanged; `_UDS_TIMEOUT`, `_uds_client_factory` and `_InterruptedToolWarning` have no changed line.

## Probe review (pre-merge)

The safety rule held in 30 socket cases (refused, silent, half answers, wrong bodies, other servers). No must-fix. Applied before the merge: a test for a first open that meets an old daemon; an own alert when another identity still answers after the restart; a notice that cannot start no longer blocks the restart; a total bound on the identity read; the notice child is reaped; `install-agent` states its wait and keeps the old hint.

## Decisions open for a veto

- D2: one notice during the wait. D3: a 300 s bound.
- The route is registered in `daemon.py`. The identity read uses an asynchronous client under an overall deadline; it runs only from the two synchronous roles.

## Not checked
