The reply sweep reads crafted mail in bounded time — PR #635, merged; part of #626.

# Phase 05.12 Plan 11: The read bounds of the reply sweep

## Result

- A linear scan refuses a text with more than 30 combining characters in a row (counted in canonical decomposition) before the normalization: `combining_run`.
- The header read takes at most 131,072 bytes and parses a field value of at most 998 characters, with a budget of 200,000 field characters for one parse: `header_too_large`. It reads `In-Reply-To` of every new row and the other fields of a row in the thread only; it does not parse the top-level `Content-Type`.
- A candidate row is parsed only inside 50,000,000 bytes, 1,000,000 lines and 1,000 part-delimiter lines (`message_too_large`), and at most 8 candidate rows are parsed in one sweep (`too_many_rows`).
- A `text/html` or `text/plain` part that declares the charset `punycode` or `idna`, or that cannot be decoded, makes its row `read_failed`. Such a part is no longer skipped.
- Every bound moves a row to `unproven`. `runtime.py`, `mail_outgoing.py` and `server.py` have no change.
- FIX-36 is not complete here. It completes on device evidence in plan 09 (M17). #626 stays open.

## Evidence

- Gate on the merged head: 4456 passed; read-only tier 4441 passed, 15 skipped; ruff clean.
- Differential tests in the suite (seed 626): 0 differences on 5,000 texts in 13 scripts, 2,000 Mail-layout bodies, 600 header blocks, 15,000 crafted field chains and 300 generated messages. A wider run through the lane's functions: 0 differences on 100,000 texts, 8,000 bodies, 4,000 header blocks, 300,000 chains and 4,000 messages.
- Each crafted input costs at most 0.65 s with its bound and 16 to 40 s with the bound removed. 63 mutants were applied by hand over three rounds; all were killed.

## Probe review (pre-merge, two passes)

- Old code against new code on 240,000 crafted row sets, twice: 0 cases where a row became claimable, 0 cases of a new `none`, 0 differences for mail inside every bound with decodable parts, 0 exceptions that left `find_own_draft`.
- The first pass found five items, all applied before the merge: the `punycode` decode (a fifth unbounded step), a race between `stat` and the read, the top-level `Content-Type` of rows outside the thread, the skipped undecodable part, and six surviving mutants.
- The second pass found no defect in the code.

## A delete-safety gap that this plan closed

Before, a `text/html` or `text/plain` part that could not be decoded was skipped. A hand draft with typed text in such a part could read as the call's own copy. Mail's own composer does not write such a draft. The part now makes the row `read_failed`.

## Known limits

- Worst case that stays: one sweep is bounded and linear in the number of new rows (at most 300). The largest sweep built in the review took 75.3 s (292 rows of the thread at the header bounds, and 8 candidate rows of 50,000,000 bytes each with a slow single-byte charset). A bound on the raw size of a text part before its decode is a possible later step.
- About 42 attachments with the longest file names fit the field budget of one parse; a message over it reads `header_too_large`.
- A charset label that Python does not know (for example `x-mac-roman`, `x-sjis`, `cp-1252`) makes a text part of a candidate row read `read_failed`. Mail writes the reply's own parts itself, so such a label is not expected in the stored copy of a reply (not device-verified).
- Text in a part of another text type (a text attachment) is not read.
- The reply flow before the sweep normalizes the original's text without this bound: #632.

## Deviations

- U+0344 counts as two in the scan (the plan's design text said one; its behaviour block said two; the probe review confirmed two as correct).
- Every usable `Received` field is parsed, as the whole-message read did.
- The undecodable-part rule, the charset rule, the read of one byte more than the bound and the `skip_content_type` flag came from the probe review, not from the plan.
